Where AI helps a growing business, and where it wastes money

AI is genuinely useful for a growing business in a handful of specific places, and a waste of money almost everywhere else. Here is the honest version of both lists.

Published 14 September 2026

Every growing business is being told it needs "an AI strategy" by someone selling one. Most of that is noise. AI is a genuinely useful tool in a small number of specific jobs, and an expensive distraction in most of the places it gets pitched. The difference is usually whether the job is repetitive and well-defined, or whether it needs judgement.

Where it helps: the first line of customer questions

A chatbot that answers "what are your opening hours" and "do you deliver to my area" at 11pm, correctly, from information the business already has written down, is doing a real job: catching the questions that would otherwise go unanswered until morning, or get a generic reply from whoever opens the shared inbox next. It should hand off to a person the moment a question gets specific or a customer sounds frustrated. It should never be the only way to reach the business.

Where it helps: the first draft, not the final one

Drafting a first version of a job description, a social post, a set of FAQ answers, or a summary of a long document is exactly the kind of repetitive writing task AI is good at, and exactly the kind of task that used to eat an afternoon. The saving disappears the moment nobody reads the draft properly before it goes out. Used as a starting point it is a genuine time saver; used as a substitute for editing, it is how businesses end up publishing something odd, wrong, or generic under their own name.

Where it helps: pulling a pattern out of data nobody has time to read

Reviewing months of support tickets or enquiry forms by hand to spot a recurring complaint is a job nobody has time for and everybody needs done. AI is well suited to summarising that volume of text and surfacing the pattern, provided a person still decides what to do about it. It is a research assistant, not a decision-maker.

Where it wastes money: replacing judgement calls

The moment a task involves reading a customer's tone, handling a complaint, or making a call that affects a relationship, handing it to an automated system is how a business loses a customer to save a few minutes. This is the single most common way AI spend gets wasted: automating the exact interaction that needed a person.

Where it wastes money: adopting a tool before defining the problem

"We should have an AI tool" is not a plan. A subscription bought because a competitor mentioned one, with no specific task it is meant to solve, is a recurring cost with no measurable return. The useful question is never "should we use AI", it is "which specific, repetitive task is costing us time every week, and does a tool exist that actually does that task well".

Where it wastes money: expecting it to fix a broken process

AI speeds up whatever process it is added to. If that process is already unclear, badly documented, or dependent on one person's memory, automating part of it just produces the same confusion faster. Fixing the process first is usually the cheaper, more effective step, and it is the one most often skipped.
