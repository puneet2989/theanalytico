Google Business Profile: the fields most people skip

A profile that is only half filled in is a profile that is undersold in local search. Most of the gaps take minutes to close.

Published 14 September 2026

Most local businesses set up a Google Business Profile once, fill in a name and address, and never open it again. That gets a pin on the map. It does not get much else, because Google's local results reward profiles that keep giving it information, not ones that were completed once and abandoned.

None of what follows takes long. Most of it is a field sitting empty in a profile that already exists.

The secondary categories

Every profile has one primary category and room for several secondary ones. An accountancy practice that only sets "Accountant" and skips "Tax preparation service" or "Bookkeeping service" is invisible for searches that use those exact terms. Add every category that is genuinely true of the business. Do not add ones that are not: an inaccurate category is a fast way to attract the wrong enquiries and, occasionally, a suspension.

The services or products list

This section is easy to miss because it is not on the main edit screen most owners land on. It is worth finding. Listing out specific services, each with its own short description, gives Google more specific text to match against specific searches than a single paragraph in the business description ever will.

Questions and answers, seeded by the business, not left to strangers

The Q&A section on a profile is open to the public, which means it fills up with guesses from people who do not work there unless the business gets there first. Adding the three or four questions a business genuinely gets asked, with the business's own answer, means the first thing a searcher reads is accurate, not a guess from a stranger three years ago.

Photos, added on an ongoing basis, not once

A profile with five photos from the day it was set up reads as neglected next to a competitor who adds new ones every month. It does not need to be a professional shoot. Recent, real photos of the premises, the team, or the work in progress do more for a profile's activity signal than a single polished set uploaded once and left.

Hours that match reality, including holidays

Nothing undermines trust in a business faster than a customer arriving to find it shut when the profile says open. Special hours for bank holidays and Christmas closures take two minutes to set and prevent exactly that. An out-of-date hours field is one of the most common reasons a good business gets a bad first impression before anyone has even walked in.

Replying to reviews, good and bad

This is not about the star rating, it is about the reply. A business that replies to reviews, including the occasional bad one, reads as active and accountable. A profile with reviews and no replies at all reads as unmanaged, regardless of what the reviews actually say.
